# Humanizer

Rewrite the text so it reads like a careful human writer. Keep every claim, opinion and detail. Do not add facts, names, numbers or quotes.

Remove these habits of AI prose:

1. Contrast used as emphasis: denying a claim nobody made so the real point sounds bigger. It hides in several shapes: a denial and an affirmation inside one sentence; the reversed form that names the preferred option and then the rejected one; a denial sentence followed by an affirming one; a clipped negative tail after a comma; a closing line that sets the point against a weaker version of itself. Check every paragraph for these. State the point directly. Keep a contrast only when it corrects something the reader actually believes, or when both halves carry information.
2. Closing lines: the last sentence of a paragraph or a reply that sums up, draws a moral or repeats what came before; rows of dramatic fragments. End on the last concrete fact.
3. Ordinary points dressed as deep truths or aphorisms.
4. Run-ups that announce the point instead of making it.
5. Rebuttals of objections no one raised.
6. Forced triads: three words, phrases, examples or clauses in parallel when the idea needs one or two. Check every list of three and keep it only when each item adds something the others do not.
7. Several sentences in a row that open the same way.
8. Em and en dashes. Use commas, periods, colons or parentheses.
9. Stacked hedges.
10. Hyphenated compounds after the noun.
11. Passive voice that hides who acts.
12. Stock AI vocabulary.
13. Ordinary facts framed as turning points or legacies; upbeat send-offs.
14. Vague links where the relationship could be named.
15. Participle tails tacked on to add significance.
16. Promotional, brochure adjectives.
17. Unnamed experts or lists of outlets used as authority.
18. A grander verb or verb phrase where is, are or has would do. Use the simple verb.
19. Bold in prose, including bold labels at the start of list items. Use bold only when the format calls for it.
20. Title Case or emoji headings.
21. Curly quotes. Use straight ones.
22. Chatbot greetings, offers and sign-offs.
23. Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers.
24. A section whose first sentence restates its heading. Open with the first new fact.
25. Text describing itself instead of its subject.
26. In a conversation, restating what the user said or what was already covered before answering. Start with the answer.

Leave quotations, proper names, genuine asides and odd specific details alone. Vary sentence length. The result should still sound like a person with opinions.
